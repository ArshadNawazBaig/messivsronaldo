# Messi vs Ronaldo free kicks: the career total and the European twist

**Messi leads direct free-kick goals 75 to 65 in the site’s 21 September 2026 snapshot. Ronaldo leads the Champions League subset 12 to five.** The different leaders are not a statistical contradiction. One comparison covers an entire recorded career; the other covers a single competition.

Free-kick debates often begin with a favourite clip. Messi against Liverpool, Ronaldo against Spain, a goalkeeper left watching: the memorable moments are easy to recall. A career record has a less glamorous job. It needs to collect every qualifying goal, including the ones that do not appear at the start of a highlights video.

The Champions League row is already included in the club figures. It must not be added again to the career total.

Messi’s ten-goal career lead breaks down into nine at club level and one internationally. Ronaldo’s European competition lead is seven. The table makes it possible to state both results without moving the boundaries halfway through the argument.

## Why a direct free kick is a specific category

A goal scored directly from the free-kick attempt belongs in this comparison. A free-kick delivery headed in by a teammate does not become a direct free-kick goal for the player who crossed it. A rebound finish is also a different scoring event.

The distinction matters because “goals from free kicks” can be used loosely to mean any goal produced by the resulting attack. That wider question is valuable, but it would need a separate definition and a different dataset. This article concerns the direct scoring record.

Penalties have their own category too. Combining the two would turn a free-kick comparison into a broader set-piece comparison without making that change clear to the reader.

## Two famous goals, two different kinds of occasion

Barcelona’s [account of Messi’s Liverpool free kick](https://www.fcbarcelona.com/en/news/1205764/messis-sublime-free-kick-from-every-angle) places it in the first leg of the 2019 Champions League semi-final, where it made the score 3–0. UEFA’s [report of Portugal against Spain](https://es.uefa.com/uefanationsleague/news/0253-0d821223b20e-6d1654fa15d4-1000--cristiano-ronaldo-empata-a-una-espana-rebelde/) records Ronaldo’s late free kick completing his hat-trick in their 3–3 World Cup draw in 2018.

Those moments are worth revisiting on their own terms. They show execution in major matches. But selecting one exceptional strike from each player does not establish which player converted a greater share of his opportunities across a career.

That question needs all the relevant attempts, not just the successful ones that remain in the memory.

## More goals is not automatically a better conversion rate

A conversion rate divides goals by attempts. Both quantities must cover the same matches. If the attempt data begins later than the goal record, dividing the complete career goal total by the incomplete attempt total produces a distorted percentage.

This is why this article gives the goal record without inventing a comprehensive career conversion rate. The source’s detailed coverage notes matter as much as the headline counts. A percentage is not more trustworthy merely because it contains a decimal point.

Even a complete rate would still combine different positions, distances and match situations. To investigate technique or shot selection properly, those differences would need to be examined rather than assumed away.

## Which answer should you use?

For the question “Who has more career free-kick goals?”, this snapshot answers Messi, 75–65. For “Who has more in the Champions League?”, it answers Ronaldo, 12–5. For “Who is more accurate?”, the totals alone are insufficient.
