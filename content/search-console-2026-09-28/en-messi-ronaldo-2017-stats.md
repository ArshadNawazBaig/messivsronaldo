# Messi vs Ronaldo in 2017: a one-goal gap, two very different years

**Messi finished the 2017 calendar year with 54 goals in 64 appearances for club and country. Ronaldo scored 53 in 60.** Messi led the goal total by one; Ronaldo scored at the higher rate per appearance. Those figures cover January through December, so they should not be labelled the 2017/18 season.

It is an unusually close starting point for a comparison. A single goal separates the totals, yet the club and international breakdowns reveal much larger differences moving in opposite directions. That is where this particular year becomes interesting.

## The complete calendar-year comparison

| Senior club and country, 2017 | Messi | Ronaldo |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Goals | 54 | 53 |
| Assists, reference definition | 16 | 12 |
| Appearances | 64 | 60 |
| Minutes | 5,562 | 5,180 |
| Goals per appearance | 0.84 | 0.88 |
| Goals per 90 minutes | 0.87 | 0.92 |

The underlying figures are recorded in the site’s historical dataset and the [2017 statistical reference](https://www.messivsronaldo.app/calendar-year-stats/2017/). The two rate rows are calculated from the displayed goals, appearances and minutes. The [2017 archive](/seasons/2017) lets you explore the same year.

Messi played four more matches and 382 more minutes. That helps explain why a slightly larger goal total can sit alongside a lower scoring rate. Neither row needs to be removed: each describes a different feature of the year.

## Club goals created the lead; international goals nearly erased it

| Goals in 2017 | Messi | Ronaldo |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Club | 50 | 42 |
| Senior national team | 4 | 11 |
| Combined | 54 | 53 |

Messi’s eight-goal advantage at club level was reduced by Ronaldo’s seven-goal advantage internationally. The arithmetic is straightforward, but it prevents a common misreading: the near tie in the combined total does not mean their output was distributed in the same way.

A club-only comparison would therefore tell a noticeably different story from the full calendar-year table. That is fine, provided the label changes with the selection. Adding Portugal’s goals to one side while leaving Argentina out of the other would not be a meaningful comparison.

## Why Ronaldo’s awards do not contradict the goal table

Ronaldo won the 2017 Ballon d’Or, drawing level with Messi at five wins at that moment in their careers. UEFA’s [contemporary award report](https://www.uefa.com/uefachampionsleague/news/0240-0e981a776a76-ff51876b4b2c-1000--cristiano-ronaldo-retains-ballon-d-or-to-match-messi/) documents that result. He also received the [2016/17 UEFA Men’s Player of the Year award](https://www.uefa.com/news-media/news/023c-0e97a48d361f-a81fefb8d60c-1000--ronaldo-named-2016-17-men-s-player-of-the-year/), after a Champions League campaign that included two goals in the final.

An award vote is not a prize mechanically assigned to whichever of two players finishes December with more goals. Team success, performances in particular competitions and the award’s assessment period can all matter. Treating a one-goal calendar-year lead as a contradiction of an award result confuses two different measurements.

It also risks reading history backwards. The five-to-five Ballon d’Or position described that December; it was not the final total either player would reach.

## Looking for 2017/18 instead?

The 2017/18 season crosses two calendar years. Barcelona’s [player archive](https://players.fcbarcelona.com/en/player/548-messi-lionel-andres-messi-cuccitini) lists Messi with 45 goals in 54 club appearances for that season. UEFA records Ronaldo as the [2017/18 Champions League top scorer with 15](https://www.uefa.com/uefachampionsleague/news/0245-0e98d71b8ca4-7d6c75ba169e-1000--ronaldo-finishes-clear-as-champions-league-top-scorer/).

Those are useful season facts, but one is an all-competition club total and the other a Champions League total. They should not be placed in a head-to-head goals row. Use the [2017/18 archive](/seasons/2017-18) and choose one common competition when that is the comparison you want.

For the calendar-year question, the answer stays simple: 54 goals for Messi, 53 for Ronaldo. The fuller picture includes Messi’s larger club output, Ronaldo’s larger international output and the different rate produced by their playing time. That is more revealing than turning a one-goal margin into a verdict on every part of their football.
