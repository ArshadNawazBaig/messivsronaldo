# Website content review and publication — 15 October 2026

The review covered all **1,065 URLs in the live XML sitemap**, the shared page templates and **102 published CMS articles in nine languages**. All 102 article revisions were published through the production CMS on 15 October 2026. Their original URLs, publication dates and cover images were preserved. The football match database was not edited.

The strongest supported problem was answer quality and placement: many articles delayed the requested facts behind lengthy, interchangeable commentary about tactics, longevity and legacy. The revision replaces those passages with scoped evidence, definitions, worked comparisons and practical explanations. Article length was not used as a quality target. Google's [helpful-content guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content) supports evaluating usefulness, originality and accuracy rather than writing to a preferred word count.

This is an editorial assessment, not a claim that Google assigned individual pages a low-value classification. The supplied Search Console exports establish the earlier performance baseline; they do not reveal Google's internal assessment or prove the cause of a ranking. The earlier [SEO audit](../seo-audit-2026-10-15.md) remains the source for that analysis.

## Publication and verification

Each record was backed up locally and compared against a fresh production revision before publication. No record had a pending editorial draft. The release used atomic, revision-checked `publish` commands and stopped on conflicts. An isolated local CMS copy accepted all 102 commands and rejected replay with stale revisions. Publication dates and image paths were checked against the originals.

Local browser checks covered 12 representative articles across every language at mobile and desktop sizes: 24 checks. They verified exact summaries and H1s, heading anchors, image loads, language direction and viewport fit. Automated accessibility checks of the article summaries and bodies found no WCAG A/AA violations in that sample. This is scoped automated evidence, not a claim of complete accessibility certification or independent native-language proofreading.

The application renderer was unchanged. Publication used the existing CMS cache-invalidation path, so no application deployment was needed. Lint and TypeScript checks passed for the editorial tooling. The live checks verified all 102 articles, including 3,828 visible text blocks, metadata, source links and preserved publication dates. All 102 article sitemap entries carry the new modification date. The 24 mobile/desktop checks also passed on production. The match ledger still matches the original six records at revision 10. Results are recorded in [validation.json](validation.json).

## Measuring the result

Use 15 October 2026 as the publication event. Compare page-level impressions, clicks and CTR with comparable periods, keeping country, device and language segments distinct. Do not join the separate query and page exports as if they identify query-to-page pairs. The ceremony on 26 October will change demand and require another factual review of the Ballon d’Or guides regardless of traffic.

The first evidence of success is correct live content. Ranking and traffic effects require subsequent Search Console data, adequate impressions and consideration of event-driven demand. A shorter article can be more useful when the removed text was repetitive, but neither shorter nor longer copy guarantees a ranking. No indexing submission, recurring schedule or future monitoring job was created by this release.
